> [!abstract] Statement
> Synthetic test cases generated by an LLM — particularly **borderline cases** configured to target the decision boundary of the current evaluation criteria — are as effective as manually curated data for refining LLM-as-a-judge evaluation criteria, and practitioners strongly prefer synthetic generation for its speed, diversity, and explicit borderline coverage.

## Evidence summary

**Single-source, moderate support** — rests on EvalAssist ([[generate-evaluate-iterate-synthetic-data-human]], NeurIPS workshop / arXiv, Nov 2025):

- N=24 user study, two tasks (bias evaluation, politeness evaluation).
- 83% of participants preferred synthetic over manual data generation (χ²(2) = 27, p < .05).
- Judge–human alignment improvement was statistically equivalent across conditions — no measurable effectiveness gap between synthetic and hand-crafted test cases.
- Qualitative benefits: broader topic coverage, explicit borderline case support, faster generation, AI-assisted editing.
- Identified limitation: synthetic text lacks natural messiness of real-world data.

The claim is **weakly supported** due to single-paper evidence, limited task diversity (2 tasks), and an acknowledged realism gap.

> [!info] Conditions and scope
> Specifically about **judge criteria refinement**, not about training data or reward modeling. The claim is that synthetic data is effective for the LLM-as-a-judge calibration sub-loop (human refines criteria → judge produces verdicts → human observes mismatches → refines further). It does not extend to downstream generalization of the judge beyond the calibration data.

> [!warning] Counter-evidence
> None directly. Plausible counter-evidence shapes:
> - Studies showing synthetic data calibrated on clean/formal inputs produces judges that underperform on real noisy inputs.
> - Experiments with weaker generation LLMs where borderline cases are unrealistic or non-diverse.
