## Definition

Self-preference bias is the tendency of an LLM-as-judge to rate outputs from itself (or a closely related model) more favourably than human evaluators would. Current evidence suggests the phenomenon is more precisely described as **familiarity bias** or **perplexity preference**: judges over-reward outputs with lower perplexity under their own distribution, regardless of authorship.

> [!tip] Intuition
> Self-authorship is not the causal variable — *familiarity* is. Because a model's own generations are, by construction, low-perplexity for itself, a bias toward low perplexity manifests statistically as a preference for self-generated text. But the same bias rewards any text that looks like the judge's preferred style, even if written by a different model or a human.
> *Source: LLM analysis*

## Formal notation

Given a judge model $J$ and an output $y$:

- Define $\text{PPL}_J(y)$ as the perplexity of $y$ under $J$.
- Define the self-preference effect as $\Delta(y) = \text{score}_J(y) - \text{score}_H(y)$ where $\text{score}_H$ is human score.
- Empirical finding: $\Delta$ is negatively correlated with $\text{PPL}_J(y)$ — judges inflate scores for familiar-looking outputs.

## Key variants

- **Strict self-preference**: bias specifically toward outputs generated by the judge itself
- **Familial self-preference**: bias toward outputs from models in the same family (shared base, same RLHF pipeline)
- **Familiarity / perplexity preference** (more general): bias toward any low-perplexity output, independent of authorship — currently the best-supported mechanistic account

## Comparison

| Bias | Primary cause | Detection signal |
|---|---|---|
| Self-preference (narrow) | Authorship identity recognition | Judge scores own-generated outputs higher in double-blind settings |
| Familiarity / perplexity | Distributional proximity to training / generation | Judge score negatively correlated with output perplexity under judge |
| [[position-bias-llm-judge]] | Prompt ordering | Verdict flips when candidate order is swapped |

## When to use

Any GE or LLM-judge calibration study must control for model-family relationship between judge and systems under evaluation. If the same base model generates outputs and judges them, familiarity bias confounds the quality signal.

> [!warning] Known limitations
> - Perplexity-based metrics require logprob access to the judge — unavailable for many closed APIs.
> - The familiarity account is strongest for GPT-4-family judges; extent to which it generalises to other architectures needs further work.
> *Source: LLM analysis*

> [!question] Open problems
> - Post-hoc de-biasing: can a perplexity-adjusted score recover unbiased judgment?
> - Model-family diversification: does using a judge from a different family eliminate familiarity bias or merely shift it?
> - Interaction with RLHF: does RLHF reshape the familiarity preference toward RLHF-favoured styles rather than raw low-perplexity ones?
> *Source: LLM analysis*
